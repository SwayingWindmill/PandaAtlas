# Local Panda Research Vault

This directory is the local-first research layer for Panda Atlas. It is intentionally broader than the current website and publication contracts. Records may be incomplete, conflicting, anecdotal, historic, or unsuitable for public release. Nothing in this directory is automatically promoted to production.

## Purpose

Capture all traceable, source-supported information related to each giant panda before deciding what the future product should expose. Collection is source-bounded rather than sample-based: a pass should exhaust the relevant facts available in the reviewed sources instead of stopping after a basic biography. The vault accepts:

- identity, aliases, name meanings, transliterations, and external identifiers;
- birth, death, sex, parentage, offspring, siblings, and other relationships;
- institutions, current and historic locations, transfers, returns, quarantine, and public debuts;
- health, veterinary care, reproduction, growth measurements, and husbandry training;
- personality, habits, preferences, keeper observations, enrichment, vocalisations, and other anecdotes;
- appearance and distinguishing features useful for individual identification;
- research, conservation, diplomacy, cultural context, public events, and notable milestones;
- photographs, video, livestream, archive, and rights leads without assuming reuse permission;
- an individual image set for every identified panda: at least one identity-confirmed image is required before media coverage is considered complete, and no more than 20 individual images may be retained for one panda;
- unresolved claims, contradictions, secondary leads, and material that needs a primary source.

## Files

- `sources.jsonl` — one source page or dataset per line.
- `records/*.jsonl` — append-only research assertions and leads.
- `collection-plan.json` — coverage goals and collection priorities.
- `reports/coverage-gaps.json` — generated comparison of media holdings and structured fact coverage.
- `imports/` — reviewed, reproducible local import batches.
- `media/` — media candidates, generated local inventory, discovery evidence, and ignored local binaries.

## Record rules

1. Every record must point to a source in `sources.jsonl`.
2. Preserve the source language and write a concise factual summary instead of copying long prose.
3. Use `evidence_level=direct` only when the source explicitly supports the assertion.
4. Use `evidence_level=secondary_lead` for news reports, enthusiast databases, search results, or other material that should later be reconciled with a primary source.
5. Set `publication_status=local_only` for every record in this vault.
6. Conflicting assertions are stored separately; do not overwrite either claim.
7. Media rights status is preserved as metadata but does not block local collection or reduce local acquisition priority.
8. Do not store copyrighted media bytes in Git.
9. Do not store sensitive wild-panda coordinates or current movement information.
10. Automated collection must respect robots rules, terms, rate limits, authentication, and technical blocking.
11. Every individually identified panda must receive an image-discovery pass. Collect distinct, nonduplicate images up to the hard limit of 20 only when the source caption, surrounding text, layout, filename metadata, or archive record explicitly confirms that the current Subject is depicted. A confirmed twin, pair, family, group, or event image may be retained without guessing the individual's position, but a photo showing only a parent, offspring, twin, sibling, partner, or other relative is not a photo of the current Subject. Lineage diagrams, transport scenes, institutional context, specimens, memorials, cultural objects, generic panda images, and research diagrams do not substitute for an individual image. When no qualifying image can be found, keep the panda marked as a media coverage gap and record the reviewed channels and reason.
12. "All related information" means extracting every traceable relevant assertion found in the reviewed sources, including identity, lineage, full life chronology, locations and transfers, reproduction, health and veterinary care, growth, diet, husbandry, behaviour, personality, appearance, preferences, enrichment, research, conservation, cultural and diplomatic context, public events, media, rights, conflicts, and unresolved leads. Exhaustive collection does not permit unsupported inference.

13. Material claims must receive a multi-source search. For official names, identity disambiguation, sex, birth, death, parentage, offspring, transfers, current location, reproduction outcomes, diagnoses, causes of death, population totals, records, and comparable high-impact assertions, target at least two independent source families when available, including at least one holder, government, studbook, archive, or research source. Continue collecting additional materially useful sources without a fixed maximum.
14. Count evidence origins, not URLs. Syndicated copies, mirrored pages, verbatim reposts, and reports derived only from the same press release are one source family. Community databases, fan archives, social posts, short-video captions, search snippets, and corporate adoption pages are valuable discovery channels, but they cannot by themselves resolve a disputed high-impact claim unless they expose identifiable primary evidence.
15. When only one primary source can be found, retain the explicit claim but label it as single-source and keep corroboration open. Do not describe it as independently confirmed. Conflicts remain separate records and are resolved by evidence quality and scope, never by counting duplicated articles. Future high-impact records use `single-source` or `multi-source-confirmed` plus a `source-family:<identifier>` tag so corroboration can be audited mechanically.

## Multi-source search procedure

Sparse or initially unnamed cases must be searched across the holder or breeding centre, government and forestry portals, state and local media, research papers and studbooks, corporate adoption or public-event announcements, official social/video archives, web archives, and finally community databases as discovery leads. The search stops only when new results are duplicates, materially weaker derivatives, or no longer add identity, chronology, provenance, conflict, media, or rights evidence. Finding one convenient official announcement is not sufficient when later naming, transfer, birthday, exhibition, adoption, or research records may exist elsewhere.

## Validation and collection

```bash
npm run check:local-panda-research
npm run test:local-panda-research
npm run audit:local-panda-research
npm run import:local-panda-research-batch -- --batch <batch.json> --output <records.jsonl>
npm run check:local-panda-media
npm run collect:local-panda-media
npm run extract:local-panda-media-facts
npm run test:local-panda-media
```

The validator checks JSONL syntax, IDs, source references, timestamps, local-only publication status, evidence/review compatibility, and controlled vocabularies. The coverage audit counts only `individual_panda` media as completed image coverage, treats group and supporting media separately, lists pandas with facts but no qualifying individual image, identifies images lacking structured facts, and writes `reports/coverage-gaps.json` for the next collection pass.

## Current snapshot

As of 2026-07-26, the vault contains **587 sources** and **3,692 structured records**: 3,619 direct-evidence records, seventy-two explicitly marked secondary leads requiring primary-source follow-up, and one inferred official cross-source identity mapping, across **295 subjects** and 37 controlled categories. The evidence layer is dominated by institutional, government, archival, and official-zoo material.

Strict individual-image coverage spans **162 pandas**. No qualifying individual image lacks a structured fact record, while **18 pandas with structured facts still lack a qualifying individual image**. Group photographs and other supporting media are recorded but do not satisfy this coverage requirement. The eleventh priority pass closes image gaps for historic Macao Shu Xiang, Chengdu Bao Xin and Fu Shuang. It also merges duplicate subject `yang-yang` into canonical `yang-yang-atlanta`, migrating two media-derived records and the existing Commons image rather than treating the duplicate as a separate panda. Ping Ping, historic Qi Miao, Copenhagen Xing Er, Chengdu Qing Qing and Xiao Xin remain uncovered because reviewed images are twin, pair, group or otherwise lack a unique name-to-frame mapping.

The local media layer contains **587 candidates**, **582 locally present media files**, and **998,129,279 bytes** of binaries. Rights metadata is retained but does not gate local acquisition. Five candidate URLs remain unresolved and are preserved as replacement leads. The largest retained individual image set contains 17 files, below the hard maximum of 20.
